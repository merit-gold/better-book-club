# Better Book Club — Content Guidelines

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the editorial and content standards for Better Book Club (BBC).

Every piece of content published on BBC should be accurate, engaging, original where required, properly sourced, and consistent with the platform's literary identity.

The goal is to build readers' trust and give authors fair, thoughtful representation.

## 2. Content Principles

All BBC content should be:

- Accurate and verifiable.
- Clear and easy to understand.
- Engaging without being misleading.
- Original where BBC is responsible for the writing.
- Respectful of authors and their work.
- Consistent with BBC's brand identity.
- Properly sourced where external information is used.

Never publish information simply because an AI generated it. Important factual details must be checked.

## 3. Book Information

Every featured book should have a consistent information record.

Where available and verified, include:

- Book title.
- Author's name.
- Genre.
- Publication date.
- Publisher.
- Series information.
- Book description.
- Official book links.
- Author website or profile.
- Relevant ratings and review counts, if used.
- Feature date.
- Source references.

Do not invent missing information.

If a detail cannot be verified, omit it or clearly identify it as unavailable.

## 4. BBC Editorial Introductions

Every featured book should have an original BBC editorial introduction.

The introduction should:

- Explain what makes the book interesting.
- Highlight distinctive story elements.
- Give readers a reason to explore the book.
- Reflect the actual content and genre.
- Use natural, engaging language.
- Avoid exaggerated marketing claims.

The introduction must not simply reproduce the author's description.

It should represent BBC's own editorial perspective without pretending to be an independent review if no review has been conducted.

## 5. Book Descriptions

When using an official book description:

- Identify the appropriate source.
- Preserve the author's intended meaning.
- Avoid presenting copied text as BBC's original writing.
- Obtain permission or confirm the applicable legal basis before reproducing substantial copyrighted material.
- Prefer an original factual summary when permission to reproduce the description is unavailable.

A link to the original book page may be provided where appropriate.

## 6. Excerpts and Quotes

Excerpts can help readers experience an author's writing style.

However:

- Use only real, verifiable text from the actual book.
- Never fabricate an excerpt.
- Do not present AI-generated text as a passage from a book.
- Verify the wording and source before publication.
- Obtain permission or establish an appropriate legal basis before publishing copyrighted excerpts.
- Keep excerpts within the permitted scope.
- Attribute the excerpt to the correct author and book.

If no suitable authorised excerpt is available, leave the excerpt section out.

## 7. Book Covers and Images

Book covers are important to BBC's visual presentation.

However, cover images are copyrighted material.

Before publishing a cover:

- Identify its source.
- Confirm that BBC has permission or another appropriate legal basis to use it.
- Preserve the original proportions.
- Do not distort or unnecessarily alter the cover.
- Use good-quality image files.
- Provide meaningful alternative text.

Do not assume that an image being publicly accessible online means it is free to reuse.

## 8. Author Information

Author profiles and biographies should be:

- Accurate.
- Respectful.
- Relevant to the book.
- Based on reliable public information or author-provided material.
- Written without unsupported claims.

Do not invent awards, achievements, publishing history, or personal details.

Give authors an opportunity to correct material factual errors about their public profile.

## 9. Ratings, Reviews, and Popularity Claims

BBC may refer to external ratings, review counts, or popularity indicators when useful.

When doing so:

- Identify the source.
- Record when the information was checked.
- Verify the figure before publication.
- Distinguish ratings from review counts.
- Avoid implying that a figure is current when it may have changed.
- Never fabricate rankings, sales figures, or reader engagement.

Do not describe a book as trending or popular on BBC unless BBC has reliable data supporting that statement.

Do not imply that external platforms endorse BBC.

## 10. Research and AI-Assisted Content

AI tools may assist with:

- Book discovery.
- Research.
- Fact-checking.
- Drafting editorial introductions.
- Organising book information.
- Editing and proofreading.
- Preparing content for publication.

However:

- AI-generated information must be checked.
- External claims must have reliable supporting sources.
- AI must not invent quotations, excerpts, or bibliographic details.
- Copyrighted material must not be reproduced without an appropriate basis.
- The founder must approve official featured content.

## 11. Tone of Voice

BBC should sound:

- Warm.
- Intelligent.
- Welcoming.
- Thoughtful.
- Literary without being pretentious.
- Enthusiastic without exaggeration.

Avoid:

- Generic promotional language.
- Excessive superlatives.
- Artificial or repetitive wording.
- Unsupported promises.
- Overly formal language that creates distance from readers.

## 12. Spoiler Policy

Book introductions and editorial summaries should avoid unnecessary spoilers.

Do not reveal major twists, important character deaths, hidden identities, or significant endings unless there is a clear editorial reason and appropriate warning.

The purpose is to encourage discovery without ruining the reading experience.

## 13. Content Quality Review

Before publication, check:

- Is the title correct?
- Is the author's name correct?
- Is the genre appropriate?
- Are factual claims verified?
- Is the editorial introduction original?
- Are descriptions and excerpts properly sourced?
- Are image permissions or usage rights addressed?
- Are external links correct?
- Are there spelling or grammar errors?
- Are spoilers avoided?
- Does the content meet BBC's editorial standards?

## 14. Corrections and Updates

If an error is discovered:

1. Verify the correct information.
2. Correct the published content.
3. Update the relevant internal record.
4. Record significant corrections where appropriate.
5. Review whether similar entries may contain the same problem.

Outdated ratings, review counts, publication details, and links should be updated or removed when they can no longer be verified.

## 15. Final Editorial Approval

AI tools and contributors may research, draft, and review content.

However, the founder has final approval over content officially published by BBC.

No candidate book, editorial introduction, excerpt, author profile, or promotional claim should be treated as officially approved until the required review is complete.

## 16. Guiding Principle

BBC should publish content that readers can trust and authors can feel respected by.

Accuracy, originality, fairness, and responsible use of creative work must always come before publishing more content quickly.
